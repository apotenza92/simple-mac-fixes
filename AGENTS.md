# Simple Mac Fixes repository guide

## Product boundary

One native menu-bar app that fixes small macOS behaviours people (often Windows switchers) find annoying. Strong defaults; every switch and setting is in the Settings window, and the menu bar menu holds only Settings and Quit. The icon can be hidden; opening the app again (not a login launch) then shows Settings.

Fixes, all on by default:

- **Cut and paste files**: ⌘X in Finder is dropped and a fresh ⌘C is typed so Finder copies and the cut is remembered; the next ⌘V is replaced by Finder's own ⌘⌥V (Move Item Here). Editing the key press in place does not reach Finder's menus. The key's target process decides whether Finder is in front, since the workspace frontmost app lags. Text fields are never remapped.
- **Classic mouse wheel scrolling**: wheel mice scroll the traditional direction, three lines (30 points) per notch with wheel acceleration removed; high-resolution continuous wheels only change direction. Trackpads and the Magic Mouse keep the System Settings direction.
- **User-facing text** is plain language for non-technical people: say what they will see happen, no OS names, jargon or system references.
- **Maximize windows** (from Macsimize): the green button and a title-bar double-click maximize; dragging a maximized window's title bar restores its previous size under the cursor; Option-click on the green button still enters full screen.
- **Window previews in the Dock** (a simple take on DockDoor; replaced the Dockmint App Exposé click): pointing at a running app with more than one window opens a Liquid Glass panel of window cards, from a listen-only pointer tap with Dock and window lookups off the main thread.
  - Windows come from the app's AX list (this space, minimized, hidden, small windows like Settings) plus CGS for other spaces (`DockWindows`); helper windows with no space are dropped. `CGSCopySpacesForWindows` must use mask 7: all bits makes macOS 27 report real windows on no space.
  - Cards: the size of Safari's preview of a tab (280 × 218 points, measured on screen), with corners concentric with the panel's. One inset of 10 points sets every gap: the panel's padding, the space between cards, the buttons from the card's top and left edges, the name from its right edge (sliding when selected and truncated), and the picture from the card's sides and bottom and from the button row above it. The row is as tall as the buttons, with the name centred on them, and the picture sits at the top of its area. The buttons are AppKit's real ones (`NSWindow.standardWindowButton(_:for:)` in `WindowButtonGroup`), 14 points with 23 between starts, the same as a toolbar window's (measured side by side), always visible: in colour on the selected card, and disabled on the others, which draws them in the grey of a window in the background. A window's buttons show their symbols while the pointer is over the group: each asks its superview `_mouseInGroup:` and is told to look again with the private `mouseEnteredOrExited`; without them no symbols show. Windows on another space have no buttons. Nothing says where a window is ("Space N", "Minimized" and "Hidden" were removed); a window that is not on this space shows its picture dimmed. Pictures show the whole window with its own corners, not cut to a fixed radius. They need Screen Recording (`WindowThumbnails`, cached for minimized windows); they come from the window server's own capture (`CGSHWCaptureWindowList`, about 16 ms a window and works for hidden apps), with ScreenCaptureKit as the fallback.
  - Choosing a card unhides, unminimizes and raises; a window on another space is reached with `InstantSpacesFeature.switchInstantly`, then raised after `activeSpaceDidChange`.
  - Above a bottom Dock the cards run in a row centred on the icon; beside a side Dock they run in a column with the first card centred on the icon. The preview's edge nearest the Dock sits exactly on the start of the Dock's name label (the arrow tip on a bottom Dock), so it covers the label (`DockPreviewPanel.labelEdge`). The label sits at a fixed distance from the icon's AX frame, measured on screen at every edge, icon sizes 16–80 and magnification off or 36–128; only switching magnification on or off changes that distance. With magnification on, the preview follows the icon frame as it grows and shrinks, and reads it again every 1/30 s after the pointer stops until it holds still. The area between the icon and the preview keeps the preview open. The preview stays below the menu bar and never grows past the screen: longer lists scroll along the Dock's edge, with no scroll bar (a plain wheel scrolls a row sideways), each preview starting at its first card, and a card picked by scrolling on the icon scrolls into view.
  - Glass: the preview's background is the Dock bar's own glass, `NSGlassEffectView` private `_variant` 3, found by putting solid colours and a sharp pattern behind the Dock and a grid of variants and comparing pixels: every channel and the blur matched, in light and dark mode (it adapts by itself). Window names look like the Dock's name label, measured the same way in both modes: 14-point system text in the standard label colour, with 13.5 points either side, 26 points tall, in the label's glass. The label sits on the desktop and ours on the Dock glass, so the label glass is `.clear` tinted white 0.32 in light mode (`DockLabelGlass`), within about 5 levels of the Dock's label, and black 0.16 in dark mode (0.06 matched, but fails contrast on a white desktop). Other cards show the name on the Dock glass alone (a capsule, a faded backing and a full-width fill behind it were tried and dropped). Every name meets WCAG AA (4.5 : 1), measured over seven solid desktop colours in both modes (darkest or brightest glyph pixels against the glass beside them): the Dock glass gets a veil (`PanelGlass.veil`), white 0.44 in light mode and black 0.5 in dark mode; worst cases 5.0 : 1 on a black desktop in light mode and on a white one in dark mode. Medium-weight names were tried and looked too heavy; the selected card is that glass as a whole, with no capsule of its own, and keeps the same room around the name so nothing moves. With Reduce Transparency on, the Dock glass goes solid by itself, matching the Dock bar, and the label glass switches to plain `.regular`, which goes solid like the Dock's label (within 3 levels in light mode; in dark mode strong colours behind show a little less than in the Dock's label). All glass draws frosted in a window that is not key, so the panel is made key when it shows, and takes key back on the next run-loop turn when another app takes it (`DockPreviewPanel.resignKey`). The first window chosen by scrolling on the icon activates its app and did this: the whole preview flashed frosted for 8 frames; now 0–2 frames (measured at 60 fps after a fresh start). Taking key back inside `resignKey` itself does not hold.
  - That glass lets the Dock's name label under the preview show through. Without magnification, after each move on the Dock while a preview shows, the Dock alone is told the pointer left (a move to a point on no screen), so it takes the label down, and a second, active tap holds back moves that stay on the previewed icon, which would bring the label back for a frame or two. It touches only `mouseMoved`, never clicks or scrolls; clicks on the icon still reach the Dock (tested). With magnification the Dock shows the label wherever it keeps the icon large, so the label stays.
  - A card's name is the window's accessibility title, unless that is empty or only the app's name (some browsers, such as Ardia); then the window server's name is used (needs Screen Recording).
  - A pointer against the screen edge is in a few-point gap outside the Dock's icons, so lookups check 12 points in from the edge (`DockHitTest.onIconRow`); this is where a pointer stops after it brings up a hidden Dock. A lookup that still finds no icon is retried every 0.1 s, up to 5 times, while the pointer stays still, because a hidden Dock can still be coming in.
  - The preview opens with no animation, in all cases: the window has no open or close animation, and a picture replaces the app icon with no fade. Hover effects on the cards still animate.
  - Speed: without magnification, the preview opens inside the mouse event, from the Dock's icon frames (`DockHitTest.appItems`, read while the Dock shows) and window lists read ahead for every running app when the pointer comes within 200 points of the Dock (`RecentWindows.readAhead`, one window-server read for all apps). A cached list with two or more windows opens at any age; `confirmShown` reads the app again right after and updates or closes the preview. The accessibility lookup still runs and corrects a wrong guess. Mission Control uses the last known answer on the pointer's path, refreshed on another queue. The Dock's settings are read at most once a second, and at once when the pointer reaches a different screen edge than the one read (`DockHitTest.checkDockEdge`), so a Dock just moved to another edge works on the first try. At start the panel is drawn and made key once on screen but see-through, with sample cards, and every app's windows and icon and the Dock's icons are read, since each is slow the first time. Measured: the preview appears 0–1 frames after the Dock's own name label, 2 frames for the first one after start.
  - With magnification on, the Dock shrinks the icon as usual once the pointer leaves it, and the preview stays where it opened, so it never moves under the pointer. Holding the magnification by sending the Dock pointer moves was tried and removed: the Dock then shrinks in one step on release, since its own shrinking runs only for real pointer moves.
  - An auto-hiding Dock is held up while the preview shows (`CoreDockSetAutoHideEnabled`), restored after a crash.
  - No preview while Mission Control or App Exposé is open (`MissionControlWatch`); one already showing closes when they open. macOS 27 draws them in WindowManager with no notification, seen as its screen-sized layer-19 window (checked on each lookup and every 0.2 s while a preview shows); older macOS draws them in the Dock, which posts `AXExposeShowAllWindows` / `AXExposeShowFrontWindows` / `AXExposeExit`.
- **Open Dock folders in Finder** (from Dockmint): only a plain Option-click on a Dock folder is consumed and hit-tested; every other click passes straight through.
- **Instant desktop switching** (adapted from InSpace / InstantSpaceSwitcher, MIT): a HID-level key tap on its own thread catches the user's own "Move left/right a space" shortcuts (read live from `com.apple.symbolichotkeys`, IDs 79–82) and, as the separate Desktop shortcuts feature (off by default for new installs; existing installs keep it via a one-time migration in `FeatureController`), its own ⌃⌥⌘1–9 (desktop N, counting desktops only like Mission Control) and ⌃⌥⌘Tab / ⌃⌥⌘` (next and previous space, wrapping, counting every space) ahead of the window server, and posts a synthetic Dock swipe fast enough to switch with no animation. The system's "Switch to Desktop N" shortcuts (IDs 118–133) are deliberately left alone, since macOS only offers them for desktops that exist. System shortcut settings are never modified, so nothing needs restoring. Never post past the first or last space (it can wedge the Dock).
- **Instant Dock**: sets `com.apple.dock` `autohide-delay` and `autohide-time-modifier` to 0 and restarts the Dock while the app runs; the saved originals are restored on quit, when switched off, and on the next launch after a crash. Needs no permission.
- **Main thread**: the active event taps run on the main thread, so it must never block. Accessibility queries from tap callbacks are capped at 0.3 s app-wide, the maximize tap only queries clicks within 120 points of a window's top (`WindowControlZone`), and Dock queries run on a background queue.
- **Volume and brightness keys** (from Simple Mac Keyboard Control): software volume for fixed-volume outputs, DDC or software brightness for external displays, per-display enablement. The overlay follows the system's: from macOS 27.2 a segmented capsule with one icon, centred 10 points below the Sound or Display menu bar icon (or Control Center, where macOS shows the missing icon), found from `MenuBarAgent`'s Accessibility tree off the main thread (`MenuBarItemLocator`, which keeps its last answer when a lookup finds nothing so the overlay never jumps) and centred along the top of the screen while a full-screen app is showing; older macOS keeps the named overlay in the top right corner.

The replaced apps (Macsimize, Dockmint/Docktor, Simple Mac Keyboard Control, including their beta and dev builds) are quit at launch and whenever they start. Other overlapping utilities in `ConflictingApp.known` (LinearMouse, DockDoor…) are quit the same way while the fix they overlap is on; Settings notes any that keep running.

Do not bring back other Dockmint actions, Macsimize's Maximize All/Restore All or full-screen mode setting, or anything Keyboard Control treats as out of scope (equalizers, per-app audio, device routing, custom shortcuts, contrast). Prefer a better default over a new setting.

## Modules

- `SimpleMacFixesCore`: Finder cut, mouse scrolling, the shared `EventTap`, feature catalog, update and setup policy.
- `MaximizeFeature`: Macsimize's interception and maximize engine; public surface is `GreenButtonMaximizeFeature`.
- `DockExposeFeature`: Dock hit-testing (it re-resolves the Dock's pid after a Dock restart), window previews and Option-click folders; public surface is `DockPreviewsFeature` and `DockFolderFinderFeature`.
- `KeyboardControlFeature`: Keyboard Control's core, unchanged apart from identifiers.
- `SimpleMacFixes`: the app: `AppModel` (keyboard control, permissions, setup guide), `FeatureController` (the other fixes), menu, Settings, updates.

Ported code came from the last commits of each source repo (Macsimize 74303c0, Dockmint 2047a49, Keyboard Control 1fffbe8). Uncommitted work in those repos was not ported.

## Safety invariants

- Each feature owns its own event tap and starts, stops and fails independently. One feature must never block or crash another.
- Permissions are requested only where a feature needs them. Accessibility covers every fix; Input Monitoring is only for raw brightness keys; System Audio Recording only when a fixed-volume output needs software gain; Screen Recording only for the pictures and window names in Dock previews.
- When unsure, pass events through unchanged. Finder cut treats unknown focus as text. The Dock tap is listen-only, so the Dock always receives the native click.
- Event tap handlers stay fast: no file or network IO; AX queries bounded by messaging timeouts; window counting runs off the main thread.
- Keyboard Control's invariants still apply: the audio path is fail-open; the real-time audio callback allocates no memory, does no IO and takes no locks; software dimming leaves built-in displays alone and disappears on exit; display disabling requires a verified surviving display, arms recovery first and is never persisted.

## Verification

Run `scripts/test.sh` after application or build changes. AppKit draws some controls (the window buttons in Dock previews) in the style of the SDK recorded in the app's binary. SwiftPM's new build system records the deployment target there instead (14.4, which draws flat, older buttons), so `scripts/build-app.sh` writes the real SDK with `vtool`; check it with `otool -l` (`LC_BUILD_VERSION`). `scripts/run-dev.sh` builds, installs to `~/Applications/Simple Mac Fixes Dev.app` and launches; keep that path and the `com.apotenza.SimpleMacFixes.dev` bundle identifier stable so permissions survive rebuilds. Do not kill the app with a blanket `pkill`: a `--display-recovery` helper may be restoring a display. `run-dev.sh` also quits the installed release build (so the two don't fight over the same taps and Dock settings) and the running apps listed in `ConflictingApp.known` (the old apps, LinearMouse and the like) and reopens them once the dev app exits, via `scripts/dev-other-apps.py`.

Behaviour that unit tests cannot prove needs a manual check on the real Mac, recorded honestly:

- Finder: cut/paste files between folders, the desktop and another volume; rename a file and use ⌘X/⌘V in the name; search field; Go to Folder; ⌘Z after a move; non-QWERTY layout.
- Scrolling: a wheel mouse, a free-spinning wheel (e.g. MX Master), the Magic Mouse and the trackpad, with natural scrolling on and off.
- Green button: click, Option-click, title-bar double-click, drag a maximized window to restore, multiple displays.
- Dock: Option-click a folder and a stack (opens Finder, no stack popup), plain-click it (stack as usual), Option-drag it; point at an app with one and with several windows (only several opens a preview), including windows minimized, hidden and on another space; choose each kind; rest on a card (the window shows in place) and move between cards; scroll on the icon with a wheel and a trackpad, then click the icon (brings the picked window forward); use close, minimize and the green button (maximizes and restores with Maximize windows on, full screen with it off or with Option); move from the icon to the preview (it stays open); drag an icon to reorder; repeat with a side Dock (one row, centred on the icon) (no fade as the preview opens, fills or closes), in light and dark mode and with Reduce Transparency, with magnification on (the preview stays exactly over the label as the icon grows) and an auto-hiding Dock (point at an icon as the Dock comes in and keep still: the preview opens).
- Volume and brightness: follow `Tests/KeyboardControlManualTestPlan.md` (from Keyboard Control) for the affected hardware.

Do not run Dock or window automation unprompted: it changes the user's Dock and open windows.

## Releases

Stable channel only (no beta for now). Add `release-notes/vX.Y.Z.md`, push it to `main`, then push the tag `vX.Y.Z`. `.github/workflows/release.yml` tests, signs with the Developer ID certificate, notarizes and staples on Apple silicon and Intel runners, uploads everything to a draft release and then publishes it (releases are immutable), then commits the Sparkle feeds (`appcasts/stable-{arm64,x64}.xml`) to `main`. The release carries an attested `homebrew-publication.tar.gz` (manifest, checksums and cask); `apotenza92/homebrew-tap` checks for it every 10 minutes (`reconcile-simple-mac-fixes.yml`), validates it against its `homebrew-products.json` entry and commits `Casks/simple-mac-fixes.rb`. Nothing is committed to the tap by hand.

Credentials live only in the `release-signing` and `sparkle-signing` environments (restricted to `v*` tags). The Sparkle private key is also in the release machine's login keychain under the Sparkle account `simple-mac-fixes`; its public key is in `scripts/build-app.sh`. Never print credentials or run `scripts/release/ci-package.sh` with shell tracing. Naming and build numbers come from `scripts/release/contract.py`.
