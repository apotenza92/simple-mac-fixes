# Simple Mac Fixes repository guide

## Product boundary

One native menu-bar app that fixes small macOS behaviours people (often Windows switchers) find annoying. Strong defaults; every switch and setting is in the Settings window, and the menu bar menu holds only Settings and Quit. Settings has the system's toolbar tabs along the top (`NSTabViewController`, the control SwiftUI's own Settings window uses), each a SwiftUI form at its own height, and opens on the last tab used: General (launch at login, menu bar icon, updates, permissions and Reset Settings), Dock, Windows (with spaces), Finder, Mouse and Keyboard (volume and brightness) (`SettingsTab`; `Feature.settingsTab` puts every fix on one tab). The toolbar sizes each tab to its label and has no fixed widths or two-line labels, so every label is one short word. The tab control does not resize the window, so `SettingsTabsController` does: as in Apple's own Settings windows, the new tab shows at once (no crossfade) and the window grows or shrinks to it at the system's resize speed (`animationResizeTime`, through the animator, top kept in place, content pinned to the top), with none under Reduce Motion. `setFrame(_:display:animate:)` blocks until it ends, and with the tab control's crossfade the two ran one after the other, showing both tabs at once (recorded at 120 fps). Every tab is loaded when Settings opens, so none waits the first time. Sections have a line under their name saying what they are for. Reset Settings asks first, then puts every switch back to how a new install starts; launch at login, screens turned on or off, and permissions stay as they are. The icon can be hidden; opening the app again (not a login launch) then shows Settings.

Fixes, all on by default:

- **Cut and paste files**: ⌘X in Finder is dropped and a fresh ⌘C is typed so Finder copies and the cut is remembered; the next ⌘V is replaced by Finder's own ⌘⌥V (Move Item Here). Editing the key press in place does not reach Finder's menus. The key's target process decides whether Finder is in front, since the workspace frontmost app lags. Text fields are never remapped.
- **Classic mouse wheel scrolling**: wheel mice scroll the traditional direction, three lines (30 points) per notch with wheel acceleration removed; high-resolution continuous wheels only change direction. Trackpads and the Magic Mouse keep the System Settings direction.
- **User-facing text** is plain language for non-technical people: say what they will see happen, no OS names, jargon or system references.
- **Maximize windows** (from Macsimize): the green button and a title-bar double-click maximize; dragging a maximized window's title bar restores its previous size under the cursor; Option-click on the green button still enters full screen.
- **Window previews in the Dock** (a simple take on DockDoor; replaced the Dockmint App Exposé click): pointing at a running app with more than one window opens a Liquid Glass panel of window cards, from a listen-only pointer tap with Dock and window lookups off the main thread.
  - Windows come from the app's AX list (this space, minimized, hidden, small windows like Settings) plus CGS for other spaces (`DockWindows`); helper windows with no space are dropped. `CGSCopySpacesForWindows` must use mask 7: all bits makes macOS 27 report real windows on no space.
  - Cards: the size of Safari's preview of a tab (280 × 218 points, measured on screen), with corners concentric with the panel's. One inset of 10 points sets every gap: the panel's padding, the space between cards, the buttons from the card's top and left edges, the name from its right edge (the name is left-aligned, 12 points after the green button, sliding when selected and truncated, and fading out over 14 points at an edge with more of it past that edge), and the picture from the card's sides and bottom and from the button row above it. The row is as tall as the buttons, with the name centred on them, and the picture is centred in its area, up and down as well as across. The buttons are AppKit's real ones (`NSWindow.standardWindowButton(_:for:)` in `WindowButtonGroup`), 14 points with 23 between starts, the same as a toolbar window's (measured side by side), always visible: in colour on the selected card, and disabled on the others, which draws them in the grey of a window in the background. A window's buttons show their symbols while the pointer is over the group: each asks its superview `_mouseInGroup:` and is told to look again with the private `mouseEnteredOrExited`; without them no symbols show. Windows on another space have no buttons. A window that is not on this space shows its picture dimmed. A minimized window and a window of a hidden app also say so, "Minimized" or "Hidden", centred over the picture in the look of the Dock's name label (`StateLabel`): minimized is one window's own state, hidden is the whole app's, so all its cards say it, and a minimized window of a hidden app says "Minimized", the step to undo ("Space N" stays removed). A window minimized from its card keeps the picture from before: the card stops playing it before the animation starts (`LiveWindow.freeze`), and its picture is not taken again (played on, the card showed the window shrinking into the Dock, then kept a tiny, blurred capture of the minimized window). The card says "Minimized" at once, and the app's windows are read again only 0.6 and 1.2 s later, keeping that window if a read misses it: read at 0.5 s, during the animation, the window was missing, the app looked like it had one window, and the preview closed under the pointer. A card with no picture (a minimized window since the app started, or a tab that went into the Dock with its window) shows the app icon with the label below it. Pictures show the whole window with its own corners, not cut to a fixed radius. They need Screen Recording (`WindowThumbnails`, cached for minimized windows); they come from the window server's own capture (`CGSHWCaptureWindowList`, about 16 ms a window and works for hidden apps), with ScreenCaptureKit as the fallback. Pictures are taken ahead (`WindowThumbnails.prefetch`): after each read-ahead of window lists, every window of an app with a preview that has no picture yet is captured in the background, apps nearest the pointer first: all of them at start (about 17 ms each), later only windows opened since, so the read-ahead near the Dock costs one check when all have pictures; a window the capture cannot take is not tried again for 10 s; it runs only after a read-ahead that read something, and pictures of windows no longer in any list are dropped then (`keepOnly`), so the cache does not keep closed windows (about 1 MB each); without this, the first preview of each window since launch showed the app icon for a few frames (pictures are kept in memory only). The card under the pointer plays live (`LiveWindow`), and its window when shown in place; every other card keeps the picture it had when the preview opened. Frames use the same capture, one after the other on the capture queue with no stop on the main thread: a capture waits for the window server's next frame (about 16.7 ms at any size, so two at once are no faster), which gives an even 60 fps (through the main thread it was 56 with missed frames). Only the window shown in place gets full resolution; the card alone gets the window's normal resolution, a quarter of the pixels to draw (the capture costs the same). The shown-in-place window takes the live frames and needs no capture of its own; of the full-size pictures taken for windows shown in place, only the current one is kept (one can be tens of MB). A frame that shows the same as the last one is not sent on (`CaptureLoop.isUnchanged`: one row in 8, a different row each frame, about 0.3 ms; a thin change such as a cursor is found within 8 frames). A window that animates measured about 20 % CPU in `replayd` and 20–30 % more in the window server (30 fps saved only about 5 % there). The card scales frames down with mipmaps (`trilinear`, about 5 % in the window server), so small text does not shimmer. Measured with 24 rounds of open, sweep across the cards and close: no crash, memory steady at 95–116 MB, CPU back to 0 when closed. ScreenCaptureKit streams for every card were tried and removed: macOS 27 shows its recording icon in the menu bar and a purple badge over each streamed window's own buttons, and 4–12 streams added 30–40 % WindowServer CPU and dropped frames.
  - A window shows in place only while the pointer rests on its card, and never a minimized window or one of a hidden app, which is not on the screen (`DockWindow.isOnScreen`); showing the selected window in place as soon as the preview opened from the icon was tried in v0.1.7 and removed. Choosing a card unhides, unminimizes and raises; a click on the previewed icon is the Dock's own and only closes the preview: a window picked by scrolling on the icon was brought forward as it was picked, so the Dock's click brings the app forward with it on top, as macOS does (bringing the window forward again after the click raced the Dock's own step and needed a guessed wait; a minimized or hidden window picked by scrolling is only selected, and its card brings it back). Clicks are not touched at all: the app sends the Dock nothing before or after a click (the "pointer left" state is dropped first, so closing the preview sends nothing either), and the Dock's name label after a click is the Dock's own. Handling clicks ahead of the Dock (telling it the pointer left or where it was, holding back moves above the clicked icon, bringing the window forward again after the click) was tried in v0.1.7–v0.1.9 and removed: it came with reports of the pointer jumping and of clicks that did not bring the app forward. The preview's window takes the mouse only while the pointer is on its glass (`DockPreviewPanel.takesMouse`, set from the listening tap on every move): its clear room for the glass's shadow covers the top of a bottom Dock's icons, and a click there went to the preview's window, not the Dock, so the app did not come forward (seen in a log of the events each process got). After a click on the icon, also a right-click or a long press for the Dock's menu, that app's preview stays closed until the pointer leaves its icon (`clickedBundle`), so it never opens over that menu, and so moving up from the icon to the window it brought forward opens nothing; a window on another space is reached with `InstantSpacesFeature.switchInstantly`, then raised after `activeSpaceDidChange`.
  - Above a bottom Dock the cards run in a row centred on the icon; beside a side Dock they run in a column with the first card centred on the icon. The preview's edge nearest the Dock sits exactly on the start of the Dock's name label (the arrow tip on a bottom Dock), so it covers the label (`DockPreviewPanel.labelEdge`). The label sits at a fixed distance from the icon's AX frame, measured on screen at every edge, icon sizes 16–80 and magnification off or 36–128; only switching magnification on or off changes that distance. With magnification on, the preview follows the icon frame as it grows and shrinks, and reads it again every 1/30 s after the pointer stops until it holds still. The area between the icon and the preview keeps the preview open. The preview stays below the menu bar and never grows past the screen: longer lists scroll along the Dock's edge, with no scroll bar (a plain wheel scrolls a row sideways), each preview starting at its first card, and a card picked by scrolling on the icon scrolls into view. Scrolling on the icon goes round the windows, with a wheel and a trackpad, but stops at the first and the last window: a scroll that starts there (after a 0.3 s pause, and before it moved the selection) goes round to the other end (`nextSelection`, `goRoundPause`, `movedThisScroll`; a trackpad moves the selection only several events into a swipe, so testing the pause before that event never let a swipe go round), and going round ends that scroll: the rest of it moves nothing until a new scroll after the same pause (`wentRound`). Going round on every notch let a quick spin of a wheel circle the windows again and again.
  - Glass: the preview's background is the Dock bar's own glass, `NSGlassEffectView` private `_variant` 3, found by putting solid colours and a sharp pattern behind the Dock and a grid of variants and comparing pixels: every channel and the blur matched, in light and dark mode (it adapts by itself). Window names look like the Dock's name label, measured the same way in both modes: 14-point system text in the standard label colour, with 13.5 points either side, 26 points tall, in the label's glass. The label sits on the desktop and ours on the Dock glass, so the label glass is `.clear` tinted white 0.32 in light mode (`DockLabelGlass`), within about 5 levels of the Dock's label, and black 0.16 in dark mode (0.06 matched, but fails contrast on a white desktop). Other cards show the name on the Dock glass alone (a capsule, a faded backing and a full-width fill behind it were tried and dropped). Every name meets WCAG AA (4.5 : 1), measured over seven solid desktop colours in both modes (darkest or brightest glyph pixels against the glass beside them): the Dock glass gets a veil (`PanelGlass.veil`), white 0.44 in light mode and black 0.5 in dark mode; worst cases 5.0 : 1 on a black desktop in light mode and on a white one in dark mode. Medium-weight names were tried and looked too heavy; the selected card is that glass as a whole, with no capsule of its own, and keeps the same room around the name so nothing moves. With Reduce Transparency on, the Dock glass goes solid by itself, matching the Dock bar, and the label glass switches to plain `.regular`, which goes solid like the Dock's label (within 3 levels in light mode; in dark mode strong colours behind show a little less than in the Dock's label). All glass draws frosted in a window that is not key, so the panel is made key when it shows, and takes key back on the next run-loop turn when another app takes it (`DockPreviewPanel.resignKey`). The first window chosen by scrolling on the icon activates its app and did this: the whole preview flashed frosted for 8 frames; now 0–2 frames (measured at 60 fps after a fresh start). Taking key back inside `resignKey` itself does not hold.
  - That glass lets the Dock's name label under the preview show through. Without magnification, as a preview opens the Dock alone is told the pointer left (a move to a point on no screen, posted to its process with `postToPid` just after the move reached it, and marked as this app's own; this does not move the real pointer, measured), so it takes the label down (`hideDockLabel`). A tap on the events macOS delivers to the Dock's process alone (`CGEvent.tapCreateForPid`, `dockTap`, an idea from DockDoor) drops pointer moves on their way to the Dock along its icons (their extent plus 160 points, so hot corners keep their moves; `dropsDockMove`): while a preview shows, every such move, so the Dock does not put the label back, neither the previewed app's nor that of an app the pointer moved on to; and a move onto an icon whose preview opens from memory, after which the Dock is told the pointer left (without this, the Dock drew the label for a few frames before the preview showed). After a window picked by scrolling comes forward, the Dock is told again. The same tap drops scrolls on the Dock while a preview shows: they pick the preview's windows (this app's listening tap still sees them), and the Dock showed its label for the pointer's place on each one, through the glass. The Dock's icons are read again each time the pointer comes within 200 points of the Dock, since the Dock moves them while the pointer is elsewhere (apps opening and quitting, windows going into it and coming back). The tap is on only while the pointer is within 200 points of the Dock or a preview shows, and never with magnification, so the Dock's moves do not wait for this app otherwise; it is made again for a restarted Dock. A preview that closes sends the Dock the real position as it closes. With magnification the Dock shows the label wherever it keeps the icon large, so the label stays. Dock previews never change, hold back or warp the pointer's own events: the taps on all pointer moves are listen-only. An active tap that held moves on the Dock back from everyone while a preview showed (v0.1.x–v0.2.0, `moveAhead`) was removed: macOS moves the pointer on screen only with the moves that get through, so the pointer froze on the icon while the hand kept moving, then jumped (measured: 32 moves held over 2.3 s, then a jump of 18 points and, after a 30 ms wait for the main thread, of 82 points). Putting the pointer back with `CGWarpMouseCursorPosition` (also with `CGAssociateMouseAndMouseCursorPosition`) made macOS ignore the mouse after each warp, and the pointer froze on the icon. Without the Dock's tap, telling the Dock after every move let the label flicker. One `tapCreateForPid` call shows as 5 entries in `CGGetEventTapList` (one per Dock connection); all go when it is removed. The Dock's settings behind `isInDockStrip` are read again off the main thread once a second (the old answer serves meanwhile), and posts to the Dock use its saved process id; a lookup that fails on it (a Dock restarted, as Instant Dock does, which macOS does not announce) clears it and asks the new Dock once.
  - Opening and closing cost, measured with a main-thread stall detector and `sample` during fast sweeps along the Dock: opening took 15–30 ms, closing 9–16 ms, both on the main thread. Now: the panel's size comes from the cards' fixed size, not a SwiftUI layout (`glassSize`), so SwiftUI lays the cards out once; cards are known by their place (`ForEach` by offset), so the next app reuses them and only changes their content (each name gets its own identity, so its slide starts afresh); window buttons are pooled (`WindowButtonGroup.take`/`giveBack`, 8 made at start), since AppKit makes them slowly (a third of the opening time); and a closed preview stays on screen see-through and click-through for 0.3 s while the pointer is on the Dock (`DockPreviewPanel.close(animated:keepFor:)`, `isShown` is the preview's state), so opening the next one only shows it again, with no `orderOut`, `orderFront` or `makeKey` (it is still key then, so it goes as soon as the pointer leaves the Dock). The panel is key only for its glass and takes no typing, so keys belong to the app in use (`DockPreviewPanel.sendEvent`): a key pressed while the preview shows closes it (modifier keys alone do not, for Option-click on a green button), and a key that reaches a closed preview's window takes the window away and is sent on to the frontmost app with `postToPid`, so none is lost. Sent back through the system instead, a key could reach the window again before it let the keyboard go and arrive after the next one (typed `abc` came out `bac`); tested three times in order. Opening now takes 7–11 ms, closing under 1 ms. A lookup that comes back after the pointer moved off its icon (judged by the answer's own icon frame, since the saved icons can be out of date or not read yet) is dropped and run again (without magnification); acting on it closed a preview that had just opened and opened it again, a flash. Opening another app's preview changes the reused cards with animations off, and clears the live pictures, so no old frame stays behind; the pooled buttons sit in a plain view of each card's own, so SwiftUI never holds a view another card took over; cards have string IDs, apart from the scroll view's own. A kept window draws the new cards before it shows again, and goes off screen early only when kept, so a closing fade finishes. With this app in front (Settings open), a key goes to its key window. The icon lookup asks the Dock's own accessibility element at the point, not the system-wide one: the system answers with the top window, and the preview's own window (its clear margin covers the top of the icons, and a closed one stays see-through for 0.3 s) hid the icon under the pointer, so a slow move along the top of the icons closed and opened the preview in a loop; the Option-click folder check keeps the system-wide answer, since the Dock also owns the desktop picture. A move counts as heading for the preview only when it leaves the Dock at 17° or more (`isLeavingDock`): a wide preview lies ahead of almost any move along the Dock that drifts toward it, which kept the last app's preview over the next icons. Reviewed by an independent agent; its findings were fixed and tested on screen (the fade when leaving the preview upward, keys in order, labels during fast sweeps).
  - A card's name is the window's accessibility title, unless that is empty or only the app's name (some browsers, such as Ardia); then the window server's name is used (needs Screen Recording).
  - A pointer against the screen edge is in a few-point gap outside the Dock's icons, so lookups check 12 points in from the edge (`DockHitTest.onIconRow`); this is where a pointer stops after it brings up a hidden Dock. A lookup that still finds no icon is retried every 0.1 s, up to 5 times, while the pointer stays still, because a hidden Dock can still be coming in.
  - Animations are the system's own, and none at all when Reduce Motion or Instant Dock is on (`DockPreviewsFeature.animates`, read each time a preview opens): the panel opens at once and fades out, as a utility panel and the Dock's own menus do, but only when the pointer leaves the Dock: moving to another icon closes it at once, as the Dock's own labels change, since during a fade the Dock already showed the next app's label under it; the window shown in place fades in (about 0.2 s) and out (about 0.3 s) with `NSAnimationContext`'s default (but goes at once when the real window takes over, where a fade would blend the two), the selected card's glass, a picture replacing the app icon and a scroll to a picked card use SwiftUI's `.default`, and a long name slides (when its card is deselected halfway, it slides straight back to its start in 0.25 s, the same as at the end of a slide; only the glass fades with the selection, since a fade on the whole card faded the sliding name over the one that replaces it). With animations off, all of these happen at once, and a long name moves to its end and back in single steps, held for as long as the slide takes.
  - Speed: without magnification, the preview opens inside the mouse event, from the Dock's icon frames (`DockHitTest.appItems`, read while the Dock shows) and window lists read ahead for every running app when the pointer comes within 200 points of the Dock (`RecentWindows.readAhead`, one window-server read for all apps). A cached list with two or more windows opens at any age; `confirmShown` reads the app again right after and updates or closes the preview. The accessibility lookup still runs and corrects a wrong guess. Mission Control uses the last known answer on the pointer's path, refreshed on another queue. The Dock's settings are read at most once a second, and again when the pointer reaches a different screen edge than the one read (`DockHitTest.checkDockEdge`, at most 4 times a second), so a Dock just moved to another edge works on the first try; both reads run off the main thread, and the preview reads the Dock's icons again when the edge from the last read changes (`knownDockEdge`). At start the panel is drawn and made key once on screen but see-through, with sample cards, and every app's windows and icon and the Dock's icons are read, since each is slow the first time. Measured: the preview appears 0–1 frames after the Dock's own name label, 2 frames for the first one after start.
  - With magnification on, the Dock shrinks the icon as usual once the pointer leaves it, and the preview stays where it opened, so it never moves under the pointer. Holding the magnification by sending the Dock pointer moves was tried and removed: the Dock then shrinks in one step on release, since its own shrinking runs only for real pointer moves.
  - An auto-hiding Dock is held up while the preview shows (`CoreDockSetAutoHideEnabled`), restored after a crash.
  - No preview while Mission Control or App Exposé is open (`MissionControlWatch`); one already showing closes when they open. That state must recover by itself: v0.1.7 as installed stopped opening any preview while a freshly started copy worked, and the likely cause was this state (hot corners open both). A lookup whose last answer says WindowManager shows them reads the window list again (just after they closed it still said open and stopped several previews in a row); while it shows, a pointer resting on the icon is looked up again every 0.25 s for up to 3 s (`lookAgainAfterMissionControl`), so the preview opens once it closes; and on macOS 27 an "open" from the Dock's notices is cleared when WindowManager does not show it (`confirmStillOpen`), since such a notice with no "closed" after it would stop every preview until the app restarted. macOS 27 draws them in WindowManager with no notification, seen as its screen-sized layer-19 window (checked on each lookup and every 0.2 s while a preview shows); older macOS draws them in the Dock, which posts `AXExposeShowAllWindows` / `AXExposeShowFrontWindows` / `AXExposeExit`.
- **Open Dock folders in Finder** (from Dockmint): only a plain Option-click on a Dock folder is consumed and hit-tested, and only inside the Dock's strip (`isInDockStrip`, from the Dock's settings, with no accessibility query); every other click passes straight through.
- **Instant desktop switching** (adapted from InSpace / InstantSpaceSwitcher, MIT): a HID-level key tap on its own thread catches the user's own "Move left/right a space" shortcuts (read live from `com.apple.symbolichotkeys`, IDs 79–82) and, as the separate Desktop shortcuts feature (off by default for new installs; existing installs keep it via a one-time migration in `FeatureController`), its own ⌃⌥⌘1–9 (desktop N, counting desktops only like Mission Control) and ⌃⌥⌘Tab / ⌃⌥⌘` (next and previous space, wrapping, counting every space) ahead of the window server, and posts a synthetic Dock swipe fast enough to switch with no animation. The system's "Switch to Desktop N" shortcuts (IDs 118–133) are deliberately left alone, since macOS only offers them for desktops that exist. System shortcut settings are never modified, so nothing needs restoring. Never post past the first or last space (it can wedge the Dock).
- **Instant Dock**: sets `com.apple.dock` `autohide-delay` and `autohide-time-modifier` to 0 and restarts the Dock while the app runs; the saved originals are restored on quit, when switched off, and on the next launch after a crash. Needs no permission.
- **Main thread**: most active event taps run on the main thread, so it must never block. The wheel-scrolling tap keeps no state and runs on its own thread (`EventTap(ownThread:)`), as the desktop-switching key tap does, so main-thread work never delays a scroll. Accessibility queries from tap callbacks are capped at 0.3 s app-wide, the maximize tap only queries clicks within 120 points of a window's top (`WindowControlZone`), and Dock queries run on a background queue.
- **Volume and brightness keys** (from Simple Mac Keyboard Control): software volume for fixed-volume outputs, DDC or software brightness for external displays, per-display enablement. The overlay follows the system's: from macOS 27.2 a segmented capsule with one icon, centred 10 points below the Sound or Display menu bar icon (or Control Center, where macOS shows the missing icon), found from `MenuBarAgent`'s Accessibility tree off the main thread (`MenuBarItemLocator`, which keeps its last answer when a lookup finds nothing so the overlay never jumps) and centred along the top of the screen while a full-screen app is showing; older macOS keeps the named overlay in the top right corner.

The replaced apps (Macsimize, Dockmint/Docktor, Simple Mac Keyboard Control, including their beta and dev builds) are quit at launch and whenever they start. Other overlapping utilities in `ConflictingApp.known` (LinearMouse, DockDoor…) are quit the same way while the fix they overlap is on; Settings notes any that keep running.

Do not bring back other Dockmint actions, Macsimize's Maximize All/Restore All or full-screen mode setting, or anything Keyboard Control treats as out of scope (equalizers, per-app audio, device routing, custom shortcuts, contrast). Prefer a better default over a new setting.

## Modules

- `SimpleMacFixesCore`: Finder cut, mouse scrolling, the shared `EventTap`, feature catalog, update and setup policy.
- `MaximizeFeature`: Macsimize's interception and maximize engine; public surface is `GreenButtonMaximizeFeature`.
- `DockExposeFeature`: Dock hit-testing (it re-resolves the Dock's pid after a Dock restart), window previews and Option-click folders; public surface is `DockPreviewsFeature` and `DockFolderFinderFeature`.
- `KeyboardControlFeature`: Keyboard Control's core, unchanged apart from identifiers.
- `SimpleMacFixes`: the app: `AppModel` (keyboard control, permissions, setup guide), `FeatureController` (the other fixes), menu, Settings, updates.

Ported code came from the last commits of each source repo (Macsimize 74303c0, Dockmint 2047a49, Keyboard Control 1fffbe8). Uncommitted work in those repos was not ported.

## Safety invariants

- Each feature owns its own event tap and starts, stops and fails independently. One feature must never block or crash another.
- Permissions are requested only where a feature needs them. Accessibility covers every fix; Input Monitoring is only for raw brightness keys; System Audio Recording only when a fixed-volume output needs software gain; Screen Recording only for the pictures and window names in Dock previews.
- When unsure, pass events through unchanged. Finder cut treats unknown focus as text. The Dock tap is listen-only, so the Dock always receives the native click.
- Event tap handlers stay fast: no file or network IO; AX queries bounded by messaging timeouts; window counting runs off the main thread.
- Keyboard Control's invariants still apply: the audio path is fail-open; the real-time audio callback allocates no memory, does no IO and takes no locks; software dimming leaves built-in displays alone and disappears on exit; display disabling requires a verified surviving display, arms recovery first and is never persisted.

## Verification

Run `scripts/test.sh` after application or build changes. AppKit draws some controls (the window buttons in Dock previews) in the style of the SDK recorded in the app's binary. SwiftPM's new build system records the deployment target there instead (14.4, which draws flat, older buttons), so `scripts/build-app.sh` writes the real SDK with `vtool`; check it with `otool -l` (`LC_BUILD_VERSION`). `scripts/run-dev.sh` builds, installs to `~/Applications/Simple Mac Fixes Dev.app` and launches; keep that path and the `com.apotenza.SimpleMacFixes.dev` bundle identifier stable so permissions survive rebuilds. Do not kill the app with a blanket `pkill`: a `--display-recovery` helper may be restoring a display. `run-dev.sh` also quits the installed release build (so the two don't fight over the same taps and Dock settings) and the running apps listed in `ConflictingApp.known` (the old apps, LinearMouse and the like) and reopens them once the dev app exits, via `scripts/dev-other-apps.py`.

Behaviour that unit tests cannot prove needs a manual check on the real Mac, recorded honestly:

- Finder: cut/paste files between folders, the desktop and another volume; rename a file and use ⌘X/⌘V in the name; search field; Go to Folder; ⌘Z after a move; non-QWERTY layout.
- Scrolling: a wheel mouse, a free-spinning wheel (e.g. MX Master), the Magic Mouse and the trackpad, with natural scrolling on and off.
- Green button: click, Option-click, title-bar double-click, drag a maximized window to restore, multiple displays.
- Dock: Option-click a folder and a stack (opens Finder, no stack popup), plain-click it (stack as usual), Option-drag it; point at an app with one and with several windows (only several opens a preview), including windows minimized, hidden and on another space; choose each kind; rest on a card (the window shows in place) and move between cards; scroll on the icon with a wheel and a trackpad, then click the icon (the preview closes and the app comes forward with the picked window on top); use close, minimize and the green button (maximizes and restores with Maximize windows on, full screen with it off or with Option); move from the icon to the preview (it stays open); drag an icon to reorder; repeat with a side Dock (one row, centred on the icon) (no fade as the preview opens, fills or closes), in light and dark mode and with Reduce Transparency, with magnification on (the preview stays exactly over the label as the icon grows) and an auto-hiding Dock (point at an icon as the Dock comes in and keep still: the preview opens).
- Volume and brightness: follow `Tests/KeyboardControlManualTestPlan.md` (from Keyboard Control) for the affected hardware.

Do not run Dock or window automation unprompted: it changes the user's Dock and open windows.

## Releases

Stable channel only (no beta for now). Add `release-notes/vX.Y.Z.md`, push it to `main`, then push the tag `vX.Y.Z`. `.github/workflows/release.yml` tests, signs with the Developer ID certificate, notarizes and staples on Apple silicon and Intel runners, uploads everything to a draft release and then publishes it (releases are immutable), then commits the Sparkle feeds (`appcasts/stable-{arm64,x64}.xml`) to `main`. The release carries an attested `homebrew-publication.tar.gz` (manifest, checksums and cask); `apotenza92/homebrew-tap` checks for it every 10 minutes (`reconcile-simple-mac-fixes.yml`), validates it against its `homebrew-products.json` entry and commits `Casks/simple-mac-fixes.rb`. Nothing is committed to the tap by hand.

Credentials live only in the `release-signing` and `sparkle-signing` environments (restricted to `v*` tags). The Sparkle private key is also in the release machine's login keychain under the Sparkle account `simple-mac-fixes`; its public key is in `scripts/build-app.sh`. Never print credentials or run `scripts/release/ci-package.sh` with shell tracing. Naming and build numbers come from `scripts/release/contract.py`.
